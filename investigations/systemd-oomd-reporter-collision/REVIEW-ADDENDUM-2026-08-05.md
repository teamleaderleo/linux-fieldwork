## Why this addendum exists

A follow-on review found a wording inconsistency between the executable reporter-lifecycle model and several summaries written around it.

The code and existing lifecycle test deliberately implement this contract:

- a newly connected generation is **pending** and cannot send incremental updates before its first authoritative snapshot;
- the still-connected old **active** generation remains writable while the replacement generation is pending;
- the replacement snapshot atomically replaces the complete authority state and promotes the pending generation;
- after promotion, the former active generation is stale and cannot update or withdraw current policy;
- if the old active connection disconnects before replacement promotion, it becomes non-writable but retained policy may remain until the pending generation commits or disconnects.

Some descriptions incorrectly shortened that to “incremental updates are blocked while a replacement is pending.” That was not the implemented or tested rule.

## Review verdict on the contract

Allowing the connected old active generation to continue is coherent and preferable for continuity while a replacement handshake is incomplete, provided the registry remains serialized:

1. old-active incrementals are processed as ordinary current-authority updates;
2. the pending generation cannot mutate policy incrementally;
3. the pending generation's first snapshot is authoritative and replaces the whole authority state;
4. no update may interleave inside the registry's policy-replacement/lifecycle-promotion transaction.

The production integration requirement is therefore not “freeze the old generation as soon as a replacement connects.” It is:

> Preserve a single serialized ordering across old-active updates, pending authoritative snapshot replacement, promotion, and disconnect handling.

A native integration may enforce that through one non-reentrant event-loop owner, a version-checked transition token, or a collapsed registry-internal transaction.
